= Epictetus on Control: Four Questions

== What Is and Isn't in Our Control?

What is in our control is our own actions — the actions that our minds generate. And within that control is our minds: our mind is a unique system because it can check itself and all other faculties, while other systems like grammar cannot check themselves. Is what I am writing good? Should I be writing this? Our mind, our rationality, and our reasoning can ask this — all of it culminating in the control center. Within this control are our desires, our thoughts, our inclinations and disinclinations, our aversions, our assents and judgments — everything that makes us, us. Things outside our control are those not up to our minds or actions: our bodies, health, wealth, and anything left to luck or another person's choice. Our actions can try to sway and improve our odds, but the outcome is not guaranteed and thus not ours.

== The Layers of Importance Within Our Control

The first layer of importance is appropriation to ourselves (the process of making something one's own). Appropriating ourselves means to take care of one's self and mind, to gain control of ourselves and our actions. This layer is related to virtue — to what is good and bad — which is what makes it most important.

The second layer is of indifferents that are preferable: things such as our relationships, wealth, health, and other such things.

== Appropriation and Accountability

Accountability is when you are in control of, or own, the outcome of something. So when we appropriate something we are owning its outcome, and that outcome is ours.

== Nature and Reality

Epictetus called it nature; we call it reality. In this world, where there is so much happening and so many chains of events, we sometimes mistake what we can and cannot control — and in reality there is not much we can control. To be in accord with nature, to be in accord with reality according to the Stoics, is to act according to our own design as logical, social beings and the roles we were given: to live under the guidance of the four virtues.
